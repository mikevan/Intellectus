# Semantic knowledge and representation

A map is not the place it represents. Likewise, an organized description of software is not proof that a computer understands it. We are investigating how to acquire reliable knowledge first, and then choose a useful way to store it.

## From implementation-shaped IR to emergent knowledge

RT-1–4 used a language-neutral intermediate representation that retained implementation structure. The experiments established reconstruction within restricted obligations, not the intended implementation-independent meaning. The current research investigates knowledge acquisition rather than prescribing a single replacement notation.

An ontology is one representation of structured conceptual knowledge. Flow, state, mathematical relations, causal graphs and temporal constraints may all contribute useful views. No predefined taxonomy of software operations or higher-order capabilities is imposed on discovery.

## What is retained?

| Record | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Observation | Directly recorded behavior or structural evidence |
| Candidate claim | Proposed relation, dimension, abstraction or interpretation |
| Declared obligation | Independently supplied requirement |
| Verification | Reproducible evidence and scoped result |
| Contradiction or uncertainty | Counterexample, unresolved relationship or limitation |
| Revision | Traceable change to an earlier candidate or claim |

These are evidence-envelope distinctions, not a required domain ontology. Distinguish what happens from why it happens; observed behavior alone may not establish ultimate purpose.

## Current and future scope

CU-1 investigates relationships spanning activities. CU-2 will investigate whether those relationships establish reusable functional capabilities. Temporal and predictive reasoning remain future research. Implementations may be selected after knowledge acquisition and independently checked, but automatic optimization and general synthesis remain unproven.
