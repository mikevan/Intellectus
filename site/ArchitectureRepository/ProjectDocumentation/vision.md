# The vision

## Seeing the whole, not just the parts

A wheel, a chain and a set of pedals each do something different. Put them together in the right way and you have a bicycle. Knowing what each part does is useful. Understanding how the parts work together explains why the bicycle can carry you somewhere.

Intellectus explores whether computers can discover that kind of understanding. Software is our starting point, not necessarily our eventual limit.

[From operations to composite understanding](vision.svg)

## Learning from experience

Think about an experienced mechanic. Years of work help that person recognize a problem they have never encountered in exactly the same form. They do not have to relearn everything from scratch.

We want to investigate whether a computer can retain useful knowledge, connect it to new observations and use it again, even if we replace the AI or algorithm that originally discovered it.

## Looking forward

Imagine studying the records of a business for several years. Knowing what happened is useful. Understanding the relationships behind those results could help explain what is happening now and explore what might happen next.

That is a possible future application of Intellectus. It is not something we claim to have achieved.

For now, we are learning to understand what exists today. Ideas about historical knowledge, forecasting, alternative futures, intuition-like priorities and creative connections remain research hypotheses. We will test them rather than assume they work.
